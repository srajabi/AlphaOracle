The dilemma: The Fed cannot cut into 4.2% CPI without losing credibility, and it cannot hike into a war-shocked economy without triggering a recession. This is the policy corner scenario described in the thesis. The most likely outcome is a dovish hold with hawkish language – raising the inflation forecast but signaling patience. That would relieve the immediate rate-hike fear but keep the long-end elevated.

Cross-asset implication:

  • The dollar (UUP) is in a strong uptrend – safe-haven demand plus rate differential support.
  • Gold is undervalued relative to the war/inflation backdrop because of the dollar/yield headwind. This tension creates a compressed volatility: gold will explode higher the moment the dollar reverses (which requires the Fed to signal a cut, unlikely near-term) or when the conflict escalates further.
  • Duration is toxic. TLT will continue to struggle until either (a) the war ends and oil collapses, or (b) the Fed overtly pivots dovish. Neither is imminent.
